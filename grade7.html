Introduction:

Kinematics is one of the most fundamental topics in physics. Everything around us moves. From cars and airplanes to planets orbiting the Sun. To describe kinematics specifically, we use quantities like distance, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration.

2.1 Distance and Displacement

Distance and Displacement are two important terms in mechanics that may seem similar but have different meanings and definitions.

Distance is a scalar quantity that refers to “how much ground an object has covered” during its motion.

Example:
If a student walks 5 meters forward and then 5 meters backward, the total distance traveled is:
5+5 = 10 meters

Displacement is a vector quantity that refers to “how far out of place an object is,” which is the object’s overall change in position.

Example:
If a student walks 5 meters forward and then 5 meters backward, the total displacement traveled is:
5-5 = 0 meters
This is because they return to the original starting point.

2.2 Speed and Velocity

Speed is a scalar quantity that refers to “how fast an object is moving.” Speed can be thought of as the rate at which an object covers distance.

  • A fast-moving object has a high speed and covers a relatively large distance in a short amount of time.
  • A slow-moving object that has a low speed, which covers a relatively small amount of distance in the same amount of time.
  • An object with no movement at all in time has no speed.

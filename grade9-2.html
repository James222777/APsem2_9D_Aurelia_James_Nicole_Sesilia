Introduction:

Magnetism is a force caused by moving electric charges. It is responsible for the behavior of magnets and plays an important role in many modern technologies such as motors, generators, and transformers.

4.1 Magnetic Poles

Every Magnet has 2 Poles → North (N) and South (S)

Rules of magnetic poles:

  • Like poles repel each other
  • Unlike poles attract each other

4.2 Magnetic Induction

Magnetic induction happens when a magnetic material becomes temporarily magnetized because it is near a magnet.

For example:

  • A nail placed near a magnet can become magnetized.
  • The nail can then attract small metal objects.

This effect is temporary unless the material becomes permanently magnetized.

4.3 Methods of Magnetization and Demagnetization

1) Magnetization by Stroking Method

To magnetize something you can rub a magnet in one direction along a magnetically hard metal object like steel.

2) Magnetization by Induction Method

By placing the material near a strong magnet it effectively gets some of the magnetism from the original magnet via induction

3) Magnetization by using electric current (Electromagnet)

Passing an electric current through a coil wrapped around iron. .

4) Demagnetization by heating the magnet

When you heat a magnet, it loses its magnetization. This is because the heat adds kinetic energy to the material's atoms, causing them to vibrate rapidly and violently. This causes the molecules that were arranged for magnetization to be disordered and thus make the object lose magnetization.

5) Demagnetization by hammering the magnet

When you hammer a magnet, it also loses its magnetization. Similarly to the heating, the hammering causes the molecules to vibrate rapidly and violently. This causes the molecules that were arranged for magnetization to be disordered and thus make the object lose magnetization.

6) Demagnetization by hammering the magnet

Placing a magnetized material in an alternating magnetic field (commonly by an alternating current or AC) demagnetizes it by continuously reversing the polarity of the molecules and forcing them into a random, disordered state.

4.4 Magnetic Field

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet where magnetic force can be felt.

  • Represented by magnetic field lines
  • Field lines go from North to South
  • Closer lines mean a stronger magnetic field

Magnetic field is strongest near the poles.

4.5 Electromagnetism

Electromagnetism is the relationship between electricity and magnetism.

When electric current flows through a coil of wire:

  • A magnetic field is produced
  • If wrapped around iron, it becomes a strong electromagnet

Strength depends on:

  • Number of coil turns
  • Strength of current
  • Type of core material

This is called an electromagnet. Electromagnets are temporary magnets. (they work only when current flows) The magnetization dissapates if no current is applied.
